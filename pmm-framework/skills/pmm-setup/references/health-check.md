# PMM health check — rubric

Ten areas, each rated on the same four-level scale. The scale is short on purpose: every level is easy to tell apart, and it fits a four-option multiple-choice question.

| Level | Name | Means |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Missing** | Not done, or only in someone's head |
| 2 | **Ad hoc** | Done sometimes, differently each time, not written down |
| 3 | **Documented** | Written down and agreed, but not used consistently or kept current |
| 4 | **Operationalized** | Written down, used by the teams that need it, reviewed on a regular rhythm, and measured |

## How to run it

- Ask in rounds of up to four areas at a time (4 + 3 + 3), one multiple-choice question per area. Put each level's area-specific description (below) in the option so the user doesn't have to interpret the scale.
- After each round, ask once: "Is there a document or system behind any of these? Share links or files, or tell me where it lives."
- **Self-ratings are a starting point, not the answer.** If the user rates an area 3 or 4 but nothing exists to show for it, keep their rating, and mark the area "unconfirmed" in the report. If an asset they share is stronger or weaker than their rating, say so gently and suggest the adjusted level.
- If the user doesn't know, record "unknown" and treat it as level 1 for prioritizing — but say that's what you did.
- Skip areas that clearly don't apply (e.g., Launches for a pre-product company) and say why.

## The ten areas

### 1. Audience and ICP
*Who the company is for, written down and prioritized.* Feeds profile section 2 · Fixed in Discover.
1. No agreed definition of the ideal customer
2. A shared sense of the target customer, but it varies by person or team
3. ICPs and personas are written down, but not tiered or used to make decisions
4. ICPs are tiered (Core / Scaling / Testing / Not a priority), drive targeting and roadmap choices, and are reviewed at least twice a year

*Evidence:* ICP or persona docs, segmentation analysis, CRM segment fields.

### 2. Product-market fit signals
*How strongly the market pulls the product.* Feeds profile sections 1–2 · Informs every phase.
1. Still searching — few retained customers, unclear why people buy
2. Early signals — some enthusiastic customers, but inconsistent retention or wins
3. Strong in one segment — good retention, referrals, and win rates in a core ICP
4. Strong and expanding — pull across segments, expansion revenue, shortening sales cycles

*Optional sharper question:* "If customers could no longer use the product, what share would be very disappointed?" (40% or more is a common benchmark for strong fit.) *Evidence:* retention or NRR, win rate, inbound share, survey results.

### 3. Competitors and ecosystem
*Knowing the alternatives and the partners.* Feeds profile section 6 · Fixed in Discover.
1. Competitors known informally; no written analysis
2. Some competitor notes or old battlecards, rarely updated
3. A written competitive analysis exists and is mostly current
4. Competitors, alternatives, and complements are tracked on a set rhythm, and findings reach sales and product

*Evidence:* competitive analysis, battlecards, partner lists, win/loss notes.

### 4. Positioning, messaging, and brand voice
*One clear story, told consistently.* Feeds profile sections 3–4 · Fixed in Define.
1. No written positioning; everyone describes the product differently
2. Website and decks carry an implicit story, but it isn't documented or consistent
3. Positioning, messaging, and brand or voice guidelines are documented
4. One approved story is used across web, sales, and product; it's tested and refreshed as the market changes

*Evidence:* positioning or messaging docs, brand guidelines, tone-of-voice guide, homepage, sales deck.

### 5. Pricing and packaging
*How value is monetized.* Feeds profile section 5 · Fixed in Strategize.
1. Pricing set once or deal by deal, with no clear logic
2. A price list exists, but discounting and packaging vary widely
3. Pricing and packaging are documented with a rationale
4. Pricing is tested, reviewed on a rhythm, easy for sales to explain, and reflected on a public plans page

*Evidence:* pricing sheet, packaging matrix, discount policy, plans page.

### 6. GTM strategy and planning
*Where marketing and GTM focus, and when.* Feeds profile section 7 · Fixed in Strategize.
1. No written GTM or marketing strategy; work is reactive
2. Goals exist, but priorities shift often and there's no shared calendar
3. A written strategy, goals, and a marketing or GTM calendar exist
4. Strategy, calendar, and product roadmap are connected, reviewed quarterly, and drive what gets done and what doesn't

*Evidence:* marketing plan, GTM calendar, roadmap, OKRs or KPO goals.

### 7. Launches
*Getting new things to market well.* Fixed in Get Set.
1. Launches happen when things ship, with little coordination
2. Some launches are planned, but the process changes every time
3. A launch process, checklist, or tiers are written down
4. Launches are tiered, planned from a brief, cross-functional, measured, and reviewed afterward

*Evidence:* launch checklist, launch briefs, past launch reviews.

### 8. Sales enablement
*Whether the field can tell the story.* Fixed in Get Set and Grow.
1. Sales builds its own materials
2. Some decks and one-pagers exist, but usage is unknown
3. A maintained set of enablement materials and training exists
4. Materials are built with reps, usage and win rates are tracked, and content is refreshed from field feedback

*Evidence:* sales deck, battlecards, playbooks, enablement platform.

### 9. Feedback loops
*How customer insight reaches GTM and product.* Feeds profile section 8 · Fixed in Grow.
1. Insight travels by chance, in hallway conversations or Slack threads
2. Some feedback is collected, but it isn't synthesized or shared on a rhythm
3. A defined process captures feedback from calls, support, and win/loss, and shares it
4. Feedback is synthesized on a fixed cadence and visibly changes messaging, enablement, and roadmap

*Evidence:* call recording tool, VOC reports, win/loss program, product feedback board.

### 10. Measurement
*Knowing what's working.* Fixed in Grow.
1. Little tracking beyond top-line revenue
2. Some metrics, but no agreement on definitions or what drives what
3. Funnel stages and core KPIs are defined and reported
4. Campaign influence, adoption, and efficiency are tracked, and reviews end in clear scale / improve / pause / stop decisions

*Evidence:* dashboards, CRM reports, product analytics.

## Turning scores into recommendations

1. **Foundations first.** Areas 1 (Audience), 4 (Positioning), and 2 (PMF) underpin everything else. A level 1–2 in any of them usually outranks gaps elsewhere.
2. **Then the user's stated goal.** If they need a launch next month, Launches and Sales enablement move up even if foundations are imperfect — note the risk.
3. **Prefer quick wins with leverage** — e.g., a one-page positioning summary that fixes sales decks and the homepage at once.
4. **Recommend exactly three first moves**, each with: why it matters now, what they'll get, roughly how long it takes, and the exact thing to say to start it (e.g., "Run discovery on our ICP tiers").
5. **Name what's strong.** Every report says what's working, so teams know what to protect.
