# Onboarding question bank

Six modules. Each takes 5–10 minutes and fills named sections of the Manual of Me (`manual-of-me-template.md`). Ask **two or three questions per round**, never the whole list. Every question is optional and can be answered any way: bullets, rambling, voice, or "fill this in for me."

Paraphrase freely and match the user's vocabulary. The goal is a conversation, not a form.

---

## Module 1 — Right now → Manual §1

1. What made you want coaching support now? What's going on?
2. What are you working on at the moment: projects, deadlines, things hanging over you? Which feel urgent, and which feel important?
3. Where do you most want help first: work or job search, habits and routines, relationships, health, home, something else?
   - If they aren't sure, offer a quick **wheel of life**: rate satisfaction from 1 to 10 in eight areas (career, money, health, relationships, family and friends, fun, personal growth, environment and home). The lowest-rated area that they also care about is a good first focus.
4. What would you like to have made progress on in the next 60 days? By when?
5. Who's already on your support team: people, professionals, communities? What does each help with?

**Capture:** priorities, focus areas, 60-day goals with dates (ask for a date if one is missing), support team.

---

## Module 2 — How you work → Manual §2, §3, §5

1. Where does ADHD get in your way most? Think about starting things, choosing what to do, time, remembering, finishing, emotions, self-talk, sleep.
   - If they freeze, offer a menu of common examples and let them pick: "spending ages deciding what to do", "great ideas I don't finish", "hyperfocusing on the wrong thing", "forgetting things on the way out", "saying things I regret when frustrated", "discounting my wins".
2. How does ADHD tend to show up for you: mostly inattention, mostly restlessness and impulsivity, or both?
3. Any recent life changes raising the load (new job, job loss, new baby, a move, a promotion)?
4. What systems, tools or habits have you tried? Which stuck, which didn't, and why do you think so?
5. When in the day do you have the most energy and focus? When do you crash?
6. Do you tend to see everything at once, the whole system, or think step by step?

**Capture:** challenges in their words with where they show up, presentation, life changes, what worked and why, peak and low windows, chronotype, thinking style.

**Watch for:** blame language ("I'm lazy", "I'm a mess"). Reflect it back without the judgment: "So starting is hard, especially when the task is vague." Don't lecture yet.

## Module 3 — Lights you up / drains you → Manual §9, §5

1. What gives you energy or motivates you? (People, deadlines, praise, values, nature, novelty, a challenge, adrenaline, caffeine…)
2. What does the opposite: drains you, deflates you, makes you avoid things?
3. What makes you happiest and most fulfilled?
4. What are your favorite ways to spend free time? Aim for five.
5. What rewards actually work for you? Do they need to be immediate or visible?
6. **Quick rest check:** for each type of rest, are you drained, neutral, full or in excess right now? Creative, emotional, mental, physical, sensory, social, spiritual (purpose and values). Give a one-line description of each if they ask.

**Capture:** motivators, de-motivators, rewards, pastimes, baseline rest table, what restores them fastest, sensory needs if mentioned.

---

## Module 4 — Who you are → Manual §6, §7, §8, §10

1. **Strengths.** What are you good at? What does your ADHD brain do well? Which strengths backfire when you overuse them?
2. **Assessments.** Have you taken a strengths assessment (VIA Character Strengths, CliftonStrengths, or another)? If yes, share or import the results. If no, invite them to take the free VIA survey at https://www.viacharacter.org/ and paste the top five, then carry on.
3. **Pride.** What are three things you're most proud of, including invisible ones?
4. **Values.** What values do you live by? What matters most?
5. **Processing modalities.** Think of a time you learned something new and it went well. What were you doing? What kept you focused? What made it click? Then confirm with a short menu: hearing it (auditory), the big picture first (conceptual), moving (kinesthetic), seeing it (visual), talking it out (verbal), hands-on (tactile), feeling strongly about it (emotional), flashes of insight (intuitive). Record a **preferred** modality (keeps focus going) and a **dominant** one (makes sense of things), both marked *(hypothesis)*.
6. **Self-compassion.** Invite them to take the free Self-Compassion Test at https://self-compassion.org/self-compassion-test/ and paste the overall score and six subscales. Don't run the scale yourself. If they'd rather not, ask one question instead: "When something goes wrong, how do you talk to yourself, compared with how you'd talk to a friend?"
7. **Personality type** (optional): if they know theirs (e.g., Myers-Briggs, Enneagram), note it as their self-description.

**Interpreting self-compassion scores:** 1–5 scale, average about 3.0. A positive subscale (self-kindness, common humanity, mindfulness) under 3.0, or a negative one (self-judgment, isolation, over-identification) over 3.0, marks the element to practice. Record the weakest element.

## Module 5 — Your stack and settings → Manual §4, §14

1. Where do your calendar, tasks and stray thoughts live today? Paper counts. So do several places at once.
2. What already helps you focus or capture ideas: apps, music, timers, walking, voice memos, sketching, meditation?
3. Run the **connection check** (see SKILL.md) and offer to connect what's missing.
4. Propose **one trusted master list** and ask where it should live. Don't migrate anything yet; just agree on the destination.
5. **How to coach you:**
   - Tone: warm, direct, playful, or a mix?
   - How blunt, from 1 (gentle) to 5 (very direct)?
   - Any vocabulary that clicks? (E.g., a product manager may like "standup", "retro", "backlog".)
   - What's your default for "done": rare, medium or well-done?
   - Is there a coach, mentor or teacher whose style works for you? What do they do?
   - Anything the coach should never do?
6. **Check-ins.** The coach checks in on a schedule. Which do you want, and when?
   - Morning standup
   - Midday "what's next?" nudge
   - Before meetings you flag
   - End-of-day retro
   - Quiet hours with no check-ins

**Capture:** stack table, connections, master-list destination, coaching preferences, check-in schedule.

---

## Module 6 — The long view (can come later) → Manual §13

1. What do you genuinely want to build toward? Not what you feel you should.
2. If that came true, what would it mean for you? Paint the picture.
3. What would make your life feel well-lived?

**Capture:** in their words. Don't tidy them into goals.

## Importing existing material

If the user shares an earlier intake form, coaching notes, assessment results or a self-description:

- Read it and pre-fill the matching sections, marking each item *(from [source], [date])*.
- If it's more than a year old, ask what has changed rather than assuming it still holds.
- **Leave out** identifiers and medical detail even if the source includes them: addresses, phone numbers, emergency contacts, ID numbers, medications and doses. If a diagnosis is mentioned, note only how it shows up day to day.
- Don't copy someone else's proprietary forms or materials into the manual; capture the user's answers, not the questions.
