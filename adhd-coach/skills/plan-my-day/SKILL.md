# Plan my day — The one-minute standup

A personal standup: what got done, what matters today and why, and who can help. Plans for ADHD brains break on contact with the day, so this plan is **light by design**: fewer intentions, real buffers, and the expectation that the day will be re-planned.

**Read first:** `skills/onboarding/references/coaching-principles.md`, then the **Manual of Me**: §1 goals, §4 tools, §5 energy and rest, §8 values, §14 preferences (vocabulary, check-in times, travel mode).

## Principles

- **About a minute.** Pre-fill everything you can (calendar, yesterday's retro, carry-forwards) so the user mostly confirms.
- **Three intentions, not ten.** Each with a why. More can live in the backlog.
- **Plan under capacity.** Fill no more than about 60% of open time with planned work. The rest absorbs interruptions, other people's requests and things that come up.
- **Energy before tasks.** A depleted user can't execute a perfect plan.

## Step 1 — Check in (one round)

Ask together, in one short message:
1. **Energy:** "Energy 1–5? Anything drained today: body, mind, emotions, social, senses, creativity, purpose?"
2. **Yesterday's wins:** pre-fill from the last retro or wins log and ask "anything to add?" Small and easy wins count.

If the user is traveling or said so in §14, switch to **travel mode**: skip to one intention and one person to lean on.

If this is the first standup after a gap of days, open with: "Welcome back. No catching up needed. Let's pick one thing for today." Do not list what was missed.

## Step 2 — Read the day (silently)

- Calendar (if connected): fixed commitments, free blocks, and the first and last free minute.
- Master task list and carry-forwards from yesterday's retro ("what do I need to plan for tomorrow?").
- §5 peak focus window and low-energy window.

## Step 3 — Choose three intentions top-down

Apply the `what-next` filter (deadlines → values and goals → only-I-can-do-it → energy fit) to pick **up to three** intentions. Write each as a short user story with a why and a time estimate, in the user's vocabulary:

> "I want to **finish the cover letter for X** so that **the application goes in today** (~45 min)."

Then ask:
- "Why are these the highest-impact three? What will finishing them free up?" (Accept a one-word answer.)
- "What do you need help with? Who can you lean on?" (People, but also tools and habits: a body double, a timer, a reminder.)

## Step 4 — Place them

- **Deep or high-stakes work → peak window.**
- **Email, Slack, admin → a low-energy slot**, batched.
- **Buffers:** 15–20 minutes around meetings and after any deep-work block.
- **Breaks and movement** are part of the plan, not leftovers.
- **Rest:** if Step 1 showed a drained area, add one small restorative thing for it (a walk, screens off for 10 minutes, a call with a friend).
- **Flags:** for each meeting, interview or hard conversation, offer `meeting-prep`. For anything that could bring rejection or criticism (applications, pitches, feedback), add a short pre-planned reset ("if it stings, 10 minutes outside, then `rejection-reset`").

## Step 5 — Show the plan

Compact and visual. Example:

```
TODAY — energy 3/5
 9:00–10:30  ★ Cover letter for X (peak window)
10:30–10:45  buffer
11:00–11:30  Call with a recruiter  ← prep offered
12:30–13:00  walk (mental rest)
14:00–14:30  email + LinkedIn messages (batched)
             … the rest is open on purpose
Lean on: a friend for an interview mock tonight
```

Then offer, with explicit consent for each: put the blocks on the calendar, add the intentions to the task list, and set the midday "what's next?" nudge. Never create or change anything without a yes.

## Step 6 — End with momentum

Name the first thing and its five-minute start (as in `what-next`). One line of specific encouragement. Done.
