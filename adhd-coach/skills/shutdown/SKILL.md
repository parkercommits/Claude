# Shutdown — The end-of-day retro

A retro that builds confidence instead of a verdict. It skips "what could I have done differently?" on purpose, because that question feeds self-judgment. Instead it asks what worked, how the user made it work, and what they learned from experiments. It also closes open loops so the brain can stop working.

**Read first:** `skills/onboarding/references/coaching-principles.md`, then the **Manual of Me**: §5 energy, §10 self-compassion, §15 experiments, §16 wins.

## Step 0 — Check the temperature

Open with one line: "How was today, in a word?" If the answer is rough (a rejection, a fight, a crash), do a short self-compassion beat first (name it, it's human, what would you tell a friend?) and offer `rejection-reset` if rejection was involved. Keep the retro short tonight or skip to Step 4.

## Step 1 — The retro (one or two rounds)

Pre-fill from today's standup and anything the coach saw during the day. Ask, keeping each answer optional and short:

1. **What worked well today? Why?**
2. **How did you contribute to today's wins or progress?** Even if it was accidental or haphazard, how did you set up the conditions?
3. **What did you try differently? What did you learn?**
4. **How did your energy flow? Did your tasks match it?**
5. **What do you need to plan for tomorrow?**

Get away from "it worked / it didn't." Look for which parts made something work or not.

## Step 2 — Wins

Pull out every win, including small and easy ones, and name the strength each one used. Counter discounting directly: "That came easily because you're good at it. It still counts." With consent, add them to the wins log (§16).

## Step 3 — Close loops

- **Unfinished tasks:** carry forward, defer or delete. No "you didn't finish." Use "moving these to tomorrow."
- **Tomorrow:** save the answer to question 5 so tomorrow's standup starts from it.
- **Worries:** park each with a time to revisit tomorrow, so it doesn't follow them to bed.
- **Experiments:** if they tried something new, note it in §15 (keep, tweak or drop), with consent.
- **Energy:** log today's energy pattern so the coach learns the user's rhythms.

## Step 4 — End with no regrets

Close with one specific, true acknowledgement of the day, and a clear end: "That's the day. Nothing else needs you tonight." If they want, offer a tiny wind-down suggestion tied to their rest needs (screens off, a walk, a brain dump of anything still buzzing).

## Patterns (only occasionally)

Every few retros, or in the weekly review, gently name one pattern you've noticed, framed as a question: "I've noticed afternoons after big calls tend to stall. Want to try a walk right after?" Never more than one, never as criticism.
