# Meeting prep — One minute that changes the meeting

Winging it can be fun, but walking in unprepared means walking in already activated, which makes impulsive comments and rough moments more likely. A minute of prep lowers that baseline. The coach makes prep quick and a bit playful, never a chore.

**Read first:** `skills/onboarding/references/coaching-principles.md`, then the **Manual of Me**: §7 strengths, §11 in-case-of-fire moves (if built), §14 vocabulary and bluntness.

## Step 1 — What is it? (pre-fill from the calendar)

With consent, read the calendar event (title, attendees, description). Ask only what's missing:
- "What kind of meeting is it?" (interview, networking, negotiation, feedback, team meeting, a personal conversation)
- "What would make it a win? One sentence."

**If it's a job interview and the PM Interview Coach plugin is installed** (its skills, such as `prep`, `hype` and `debrief`, are available), ask before doing anything else:

> "This looks like a job interview. Do you want the full **PM Interview Coach** prep (interviewer research, likely questions mapped to your storybank, concerns and a day-of cheat sheet), or stick with a quick **meeting-prep** card here?"

- **PM Interview Coach:** hand off to its `prep` skill (or `hype` if the interview is within the hour), passing along what you already know: company, role, time, interviewers. Then step back; offer a calming move or a `rejection-reset` plan only if the user wants one.
- **Meeting prep:** carry on below. Don't ask again in this chat.
- If PM Interview Coach isn't installed, carry on below without mentioning it.

Choose the depth: **one-minute prep** (default, or if the meeting is soon) or **full prep** (for interviews and high-stakes conversations, when there's time).

## Step 2 — One-minute prep

Produce a short card, no more than a phone screen:

> **Goal:** [one sentence]
> **They care about:** [1–2 lines per key person: what motivates them, what worries them]
> **Likely pushback or hard question:** [1–3]
> **Have handy:** [1–3 data points, examples or numbers]
> **If it gets tense:** ["yes, if…" phrasing, an empathetic acknowledgement, then the data]
> **Connection point:** [one human thing to open with or ask about]
> **Calming move:** [from §11, or: two slow breaths, feet on the floor, hands on thighs]

For anything you research (a company, a person's public role), use only public, relevant information and say where it came from. Don't speculate about people's private lives.

## Step 3 — Full prep (interview mode)

Add:
1. **The role and company in two lines**, from their public site and the job description (ask the user to paste it).
2. **Likely questions** (5–7), including at least one about failure or conflict.
3. **Stories:** map the user's best stories to those questions in a short situation, action, result format. Draw on §7 strengths, the wins log and `best-self` themes. Translate big-picture, systems-level answers into a step-by-step story for linear listeners: headline first, then three beats, then the result.
4. **Questions to ask them** (2–3) that show judgment, not just interest.
5. **The awkward bits**: gaps, being "overqualified", salary. Plan a calm, honest line for each.
6. **A practice round** if they want it: the coach asks two questions and gives quick, kind, specific feedback.

## Step 4 — Plan for a sting

Some meetings bring criticism or rejection. Add one line: "If it stings, step outside for five minutes and then we'll do a reset." Remind them not to send any follow-up message while activated.

## Step 5 — Debrief (after, two minutes)

When the user comes back, or at a scheduled check-in after the meeting. (If it was a job interview prepped with PM Interview Coach, offer its `debrief` instead, which logs the interview to the user's Coaching State. Still offer `rejection-reset` if anything stung.)
- "What went well? What did you do that helped?" Log wins with consent.
- "Anything that stung?" If yes, offer `rejection-reset` now.
- "Any follow-ups?" Turn them into tasks, and draft any thank-you note for the user to review and send themselves.
